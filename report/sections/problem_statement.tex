\section{Problem Statement}

Given a Cell Painting image $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{5 \times H \times W}$ (five fluorescence channels, acquired at $1080 \times 1080$ pixels and resized to $384 \times 384$ for the backbone) and a text description $t$ of the perturbation applied to the imaged cells, we learn encoders $f_{\text{img}}$ and $f_{\text{txt}}$ that map both into a shared unit sphere:
\begin{equation}
    f_{\text{img}}(\mathbf{x}), \; f_{\text{txt}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^d, \quad \|f_{\text{img}}(\mathbf{x})\|_2 = \|f_{\text{txt}}(t)\|_2 = 1.
\end{equation}
Matching image--text pairs should have high cosine similarity and non-matching pairs low similarity. We use $d = 512$. In practice the image unit is a well: all sites of one well are encoded together and produce one vector.

The trained encoders support three retrieval tasks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Text-to-image retrieval.} Given a perturbation description, retrieve the wells that show the corresponding phenotype.
    \item \textbf{Image-to-text retrieval.} Given a well, return the perturbation description that best matches its morphology.
    \item \textbf{Image-to-image retrieval.} Compare well embeddings by cosine similarity, without text at inference time. This is the setting of the standard CPJUMP1 benchmark: do replicate wells of one perturbation match, and do perturbations that share a target match across modalities?
\end{itemize}

Three properties of the data make this harder than natural image--caption alignment.

\paragraph{Batch effects.} The same perturbation looks different across plates and imaging days. Illumination, staining and focus drift add technical variance that is often larger than the biological signal. In CPJUMP1, replicability depends on modality (compounds replicate more cleanly than CRISPR) and on cell line (U2OS against A549)~\cite{chandrasekaran2024jump}, and the distribution of per-perturbation average precision is heavily right-skewed: most perturbations score close to zero.

\paragraph{Fuzzy ground truth.} Perturbations do not come with clean positive and negative labels. Two compounds may share a primary target and diverge on secondary effects. A CRISPR knockout and a small molecule can converge on one pathway through different mechanisms. Similarity is a continuum, which argues for soft-positive losses over hard-negative InfoNCE.

\paragraph{Scale and compute.} CPJUMP1 has 51 plates and 3.31\,TiB of raw 16-bit TIFF images, about 3 million fields of view and 75 million cells. Training full vision and language encoders end to end at this scale is out of reach on one consumer GPU. We instead run the frozen backbones once, cache their outputs, and train only the small components that sit on top.
