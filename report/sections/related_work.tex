\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Morphological Profiling}

Cell Painting~\cite{bray2016cellpainting} fixed the five-stain protocol that most high-content morphological profiling now uses~\cite{seal2024cellpainting}. The classical analysis tool, CellProfiler~\cite{carpenter2006cellprofiler}, measures more than 1,000 handcrafted features per cell. On CPJUMP1~\cite{chandrasekaran2024jump} that pipeline retrieves 5 to 25\% of the expected sister perturbations within a modality and 7 to 17\% of CRISPR guide matches, and cross-modal compound-to-gene matching is harder still.

Handcrafted pipelines encode a fixed set of measurements. Self-supervised vision transformers trained with DINO outperform CellProfiler on drug-target and gene-family classification with zero-shot transfer~\cite{moshkov2025ssl}, motivating our use of frozen foundation-model features.

\subsection{Contrastive Learning for Cell Painting}

CLOOME~\cite{sanchez2023cloome} was the first CLIP-style model for Cell Painting. It trains a ResNet image encoder against a Morgan-fingerprint MLP and reaches top-1 retrieval more than $70\times$ random over about 2,000 candidates. It handles chemical perturbations only and treats the five channels as RGB.

MolPhenix~\cite{fradkin2024molphenix} swaps the trained image branch for a frozen Phenom-1 encoder, averages replicate embeddings, and adds a soft-to-label loss, gaining $8.78\times$ over CLOOME. It also covers compounds only and depends on a proprietary backbone.

CellCLIP~\cite{lu2025cellclip} is the closest prior work. It uses templated prompts (``A Cell Painting image of [cell\_type] cells treated with [drug\_name], SMILES: [SMILES\_string]''), a CrossChannelFormer that encodes each channel separately before attending across channels, attention-based pooling across cells, and a Continuously Weighted Contrastive Loss (CWCL) for soft positives. It runs on DINOv2-g (1.1B parameters, 1.48B in total) and is evaluated on the Bray dataset~\cite{ljosa2012annotated} and on RxRx3-core gene--gene benchmarks (CORUM recall 0.354). Its text branch admits every perturbation type, but its cross-class mAP falls below the CellProfiler baseline, so text supervision alone does not solve the batch-effect problem.

\subsection{Batch-Effect Correction}

CWA-MSN~\cite{huang2025cwamsn} addresses batch effects inside a masked siamese network by aligning embeddings of cells that received the same perturbation across wells and plates. It absorbs technical variance without an explicit batch label. With 22M parameters and 0.2M training images it improves on CellCLIP by 9\% and on OpenPhenom by 29\% on gene--gene retrieval (CORUM recall 0.386). It has no text branch.

\subsection{Foundation Models for Microscopy}

DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} and DINOv3~\cite{simeoni2025dinov3} give self-supervised visual features that transfer to biological imaging despite natural-image pretraining~\cite{moshkov2025ssl}. DINOv3 adds gram anchoring and a revised training recipe. Channel Vision Transformers~\cite{bao2024channelvit} show that per-channel encoding followed by cross-channel attention works for multi-channel microscopy, which is the pattern our channel aggregator follows.

On the text side, BioClinical ModernBERT~\cite{sounack2025bioclinical} (150M parameters) is pre-trained on 53.5B tokens of PubMed, PMC and clinical text, about $17\times$ the biomedical corpus behind the PubMedBERT (110M, 3.1B tokens) that CellCLIP uses. It is built on ModernBERT (rotary embeddings, Flash Attention, GeGLU) and can encode both small-molecule and gene descriptions with one model, unlike SMILES-specialized encoders such as ChemBERTa.
