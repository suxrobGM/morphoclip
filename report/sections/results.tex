\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

Unless otherwise noted, results come from a single training seed. Retrieval is measured on the held-out splits described in Section~\ref{sec:protocol}, while the standard benchmark uses its eligible CPJUMP1 plates. Chance performance is shown alongside each retrieval result.

At the perturbation level, the correct match appears in the top ten for roughly two out of five queries in either direction. This is more than three times the expected chance rate. No single addition improves retrieval consistently across validation and test data. The ranking of the variants changes between splits, and a difference of several percentage points represents only a few perturbations. \paragraph{Replicability.} The replicate loss improves mAP on all twelve tracks, increasing pooled mAP from 0.298 to 0.343. The combined model reaches 0.369, while plate offsets alone do not improve the pooled result. This is the clearest change in the study, but it is also closely tied to the training objective: the loss explicitly pulls replicate wells together, and the benchmark measures how well those wells retrieve one another.

\paragraph{Target matching.} No variant is consistently better on the larger compound tracks. The smaller CRISPR tracks are especially sensitive to the benchmark's significance test. Overall, the target-matching results do not show that replicate alignment or plate correction improves relationships beyond perturbation identity.

\paragraph{Gene--compound matching.} Cross-modality retrieval remains weak for every variant. Almost no gene--compound relationships pass the significance threshold, and pooled mAP shows no consistent ordering. The tested training changes therefore do not solve the central challenge of linking compounds to genetic perturbations with the same target.

\subsection{Comparison with Baselines}

\paragraph{CellProfiler.} The base model's mean result across the four compound target-matching tracks falls within the published CellProfiler range~\cite{chandrasekaran2024jump}. Because we did not rerun CellProfiler under the same pipeline, this comparison provides context but does not establish an advantage.

\paragraph{CellCLIP.} Table~\ref{tab:cellclip} places the released CellCLIP checkpoint in the same benchmark harness. The comparison is not fully controlled: CellCLIP uses an additional KernelPCA correction, and fraction retrieved varies with the benchmark's permutation test. The table should therefore be read as a reference rather than a ranking.

\paragraph{CWA-MSN.} CWA-MSN is evaluated on different gene--gene benchmarks, so a direct numerical comparison is not possible. Conceptually, its alignment of repeated perturbations is closer to our replicate loss than to plate correction, which is consistent with the component that improved replicability here.

\subsection{Cached Feature Analysis}

We checked whether channel identity survives the frozen backbone using 500 sites sampled with seed 42 from plate BR00116991. The reproducible diagnostic output is stored with the figure sources. A variance decomposition attributes 31.1\% of variance to between-channel differences, 37.1\% to between-site differences and 31.8\% to the residual (Figure~\ref{fig:variance}). Same-channel tokens from different sites have mean cosine similarity 0.84, while cross-channel tokens from different sites average 0.75 (Figure~\ref{fig:channelsim}). The two-dimensional PCA projection in Figure~\ref{fig:pca} provides a qualitative view of this channel structure.
