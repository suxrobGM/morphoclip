\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

MorphoCLIP maps Cell Painting wells and text descriptions of perturbations into one 512-dimensional space with about 14M trained parameters on top of two frozen backbones. On CPJUMP1 it retrieves the right text for a held-out perturbation profile, or the right profile for a text, within the top ten of 98 candidates about 39\% of the time on validation and 37 to 44\% on test, against a chance rate of 10 to 12\%. One model handles compounds, CRISPR knockouts and ORF overexpressions. On the standard benchmark its compound replicability is level with or above the released CellCLIP checkpoint on the same harness, at one hundredth of that model's size, and its compound target matching sits inside the published CellProfiler range.

The ablations are more modest than we hoped when we designed them. Gene-aware soft labels, the replicate alignment loss and condition-relative plate offsets each change validation retrieval by a few perturbations, and the test split reorders them. What survives is that the replicate loss raises benchmark replicability on every track and in the deterministic metric, and that the naive in-batch plate correction is harmful for a reason we can now state: on CPJUMP1 the plate mean carries the condition, and the prompt describes the condition. The condition-relative offset removes that failure without, so far, buying anything measurable at retrieval or on the benchmark. With all three additions on, replicability is the highest we measured (mean mAP 0.37 against 0.30 for the base model), and retrieval is among the best in the campaign without being additive.

Cross-modality gene--compound matching, the task this line of work exists for, is at or near zero for every model we trained: at most three compound--gene pairs pass the significance gate in any run, and we have no positive result on it.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}

Every retrieval number in this paper is from one seed, and the split-to-split movement of a single checkpoint is as large as any ablation effect. Seeds are the first thing to add, for the control and for the replicate-loss and combined runs. Fraction retrieved on the standard benchmark varies between runs of the same code, so those tables should be read on the mAP columns and treated as indicative on the others. We did not rerun CellProfiler on our harness, and the CellCLIP row comes from a separate run with a different export path and, in its own recipe, a batch-correction step ours lacks. The replicability gain from the replicate loss is in part the loss doing what it says, and a claim that the embedding is more informative needs a task the loss does not optimize, which target matching did not deliver at this seed count.

On cross-modality, one concrete lead is the choice of gene annotation. Training soft labels use each compound's primary target (160 distinct genes) while the benchmark's gene--compound track uses the full target list (758 distinct genes, off-targets included). Aligning the two, or training on the wider list, is a one-line change we have not measured. A second lead is prompt content: whether the SMILES string and gene-function text carry signal through BioClinical ModernBERT, or are inert, can be tested by ablating them out of the templates. Beyond CPJUMP1, the full JUMP corpus of about 140,000 perturbations across many sites and cell lines is the setting in which a text-supervised model would have to prove that its embeddings transfer.

We release the code, configs, split manifests and the evaluation harness with the paper.
