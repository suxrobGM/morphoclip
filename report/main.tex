\begin{abstract}
  Cell Painting microscopy captures how cells change after a chemical or genetic perturbation. Connecting these images to the perturbations that produced them could make large imaging screens easier to search and interpret, but the task remains difficult because biological effects are subtle and technical variation is substantial.
  We introduce MorphoCLIP, a contrastive model that links Cell Painting profiles with text descriptions of compounds, CRISPR knockouts and ORF overexpressions. The model uses frozen vision and language backbones and trains only a compact cross-channel module and projection layers, making it practical to train on a single consumer GPU.
  On held-out CPJUMP1 data, MorphoCLIP retrieves the correct perturbation within its top ten results substantially more often than chance in both image-to-text and text-to-image searches. Adding a replicate-alignment loss makes profiles from repeated experiments more consistent, although this improvement does not yet translate into reliable gene--compound matching. Gene-aware labels and plate correction also show no consistent retrieval benefit in our single-seed experiments.
  These findings suggest that text supervision is a useful way to organize mixed chemical and genetic Cell Painting data, while also showing that cross-modality matching remains an open challenge.
\end{abstract}
